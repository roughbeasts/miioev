Consumer warning

Before you download the Miio EV charging app — read the reviews first.

Miio (used across Portugal, Spain, and France) has a documented pattern of complaints about non-refundable prepaid balances, billing for sessions that never actually charged, and unresponsive support — including a case where the Portuguese Consumer Authority couldn't get a refund issued.

What's actually documented

“

Miio doesn't charge per kWh at the point of use — it deducts from a prepaid "wallet." Users report that unused wallet funds are effectively non-refundable and can only be spent inside the app, even after the rental car or trip is long over.

Source: user complaint, travelinti.com (2025)

A user reported the app showed "not connected, not charging" for an entire session, tried for 40+ minutes to stop it through every method available, and was still invoiced for the session afterward. Miio's own support reportedly acknowledged similar cases from other users.

Source: App Store review, apps.apple.com/miio

A Trustpilot reviewer paid Miio to activate a charger that turned out to be non-functional, was told by Miio's own helpline they were aware of the fault, and received written agreement from Miio to refund the payment — which was never actually paid out. The Portuguese Consumer Authority contacted Miio on the customer's behalf and reportedly got no response either.

Source: Trustpilot review of miio.com

Multiple reviewers describe being charged more than the energy actually delivered (one cites a charge of €12.50 for a session their car's own app measured at 7.7 kW), and describe support as effectively unreachable — invalid contact email, no resolution after hours of waiting.

Source: App Store & justuseapp.com aggregated reviews
On the "locks your car" concern: I couldn't independently verify that Miio specifically causes a car to become locked or undriveable. What is documented — including in Miio's own App Store changelog — is a pattern of "stuck cable" issues, where the charging cable itself won't release from the vehicle. This is a known failure mode across several EV charging apps (not unique to Miio) and is usually caused by a session the app thinks is still active, a communication glitch with the charging station, or a frozen/faulty latch — not necessarily the app deliberately locking anything. If you've experienced your car being immobilized rather than just a stuck cable, that's worth reporting directly, since it goes beyond what's publicly documented.

If you're stuck with a cable that won't release

  • Confirm the session is actually stopped in the app — not just showing "not charging."
  • Try locking, then unlocking the car with the key fob (releases many stuck latches).
  • Check your vehicle's manual for the manual/emergency cable release, often in the trunk near the charge port.
  • Don't force the connector — it's a common way to damage the charge port itself.

If you've already lost money to Miio

  • Screenshot everything: the session, the charge, any support replies.
  • Dispute the charge with your card issuer/bank directly — this tends to get faster results than app support.
  • In Portugal, you can file with DECO (Associação Portuguesa para a Defesa do Consumidor) or the Direção-Geral do Consumidor.
  • Leave a dated, factual review (App Store, Google Play, Trustpilot) — it's part of what surfaced the pattern above.

Report the app to Apple / Google

Apple App Store — reports go through Apple's dedicated problem-reporting site, separate from a normal review.

  1. Go to reportaproblem.apple.com and sign in with the Apple ID you used.
  2. Click "I'd like to" and choose "Report a scam or fraud" (use "Report a quality issue" if you didn't lose money, just had a bad experience).
  3. Select the Miio app from your purchase/download history, describe what happened, and submit.
  4. If you also lost money on an in-app purchase (wallet top-up), you can separately choose "Request a refund" in the same "I'd like to" menu.
App listing: apps.apple.com/miio · Official guidance: support.apple.com/122712

Google Play Store — flagging is built into the app listing itself.

  1. Open the Miio listing: play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.muvext.miio
  2. Tap the three-dot menu (⋮) near the top of the listing.
  3. Select "Flag as inappropriate."
  4. Choose the closest reason — "Harmful to device or data" if you believe there's a technical/security issue, or "Other objection" for billing/fraud complaints — and add details.
  5. Submit. Google won't send an individual reply, but flags feed into their review process.
Official guidance: support.google.com/googleplay/answer/2853570

Also worth doing, in parallel:

  • Leave a dated, factual written review on the app's store page — reviews carry more public weight than a backend flag.
  • If you're in Portugal: file with Livro de Reclamações or DECO.
  • Dispute the charge directly with your card issuer — chargebacks tend to move faster than any app-store report.
